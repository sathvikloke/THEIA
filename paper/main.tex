{\large\bfseries Supervised Tumor Attention Transfers Across CT Cohorts, but
\textit{EGFR} Prediction Does Not Exceed Clinical Variables\par}

\textbf{Article type:} Original Research

\textbf{Summary statement.} \textbf{Mask-supervised tumor attention transferred to
an external cohort in another country, whereas prediction of epidermal growth
factor receptor mutation status from the same images did not exceed a
five-variable model of routine clinical data.}

\textbf{Key points}

\begin{itemize}\itemsep0pt
  \item In 153 patients with a known \textit{EGFR} call, pooled out-of-fold area
    under the receiver operating characteristic curve was $0.618 \pm 0.025$,
    compared with 0.794 for smoking status alone; adding the model to a
    five-variable clinical model changed it by $-0.029 \pm 0.015$, with every
    seed's confidence interval including zero.
  \item Mask-supervised attention transferred to 420 external patients from
    another country and scanner fleet. Because the crops are lesion-centered, a
    fixed center prior already reaches 0.270 of 0.292 on attention mass; the
    informative margins are pointing accuracy 0.609 versus 0.476 and area-matched
    overlap 0.394 versus 0.287, both invariant to the prior's width.
  \item Across six defensible preprocessing choices the radiomics comparator
    moved 0.136 in discrimination, more than the effect sizes this literature
    reports as findings.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Keywords:} CT; Lung; Radiogenomics; Supervised Learning; Technology
Assessment.

\textbf{Abbreviations:} AUC = area under the receiver operating characteristic
curve, CI = confidence interval, CV = cross-validation, IQR = interquartile
range, NSCLC = non--small cell lung cancer, ROI = region of interest,
TKI = tyrosine kinase inhibitor.

{\large\bfseries Abstract\par}

\textbf{Purpose:} To evaluate whether a vision encoder with mask-supervised
attention predicts \textit{EGFR} mutation status from lung CT beyond routine
clinical variables, and whether its attention stays aligned to the tumor on an
unseen cohort.

\textbf{Materials and Methods:} This retrospective secondary analysis used the
public NSCLC-Radiogenomics collection, whose CT dates are de-identification
offsets, so no accrual window is recoverable. Of 211 patients,
153 had a known \textit{EGFR} call (mean age, 69 years $\pm$ 9 [SD]; 98 male, 55
female; 40 mutant [26.1\%]). A BiomedCLIP ViT-B/16 encoder with mask-supervised
region attention was trained with nested five-fold cross-validation, three seeds.
Inputs were lesion-centered crops, so the endpoint measures attention alignment,
not detection. That endpoint was attention-mass lift over a per-fold spatial
shuffle on 420 held-out NSCLC-Radiomics patients, with a center prior and
untrained head as controls. A co-primary endpoint, incremental area under the receiver operating
characteristic curve (AUC) over a five-variable clinical model, was pre-declared
as an expected null. \textit{P} values less than .05 indicated significance.

\textbf{Results:} Pooled out-of-fold \textit{EGFR} AUC was $0.618 \pm 0.025$
against 0.794 for smoking status alone. Adding the model to clinical
variables changed AUC by $-0.029 \pm 0.015$; the best fusion reached
0.736 $\pm$ 0.005, below the clinical model. Calibration slope was 0.19, and
Brier score exceeded the base-rate floor (0.236 vs 0.193). Attention transferred:
mass lift was $+0.244$ over the shuffle in 11 of 15 evaluations. A center prior
reached 0.270 of the model's 0.292 on mass, but pointing accuracy was 0.609
versus 0.476 and overlap 0.394 versus 0.287.

\textbf{Conclusion:} Mask-supervised tumor attention transferred across
institutions, whereas lesion-anchored \textit{EGFR} prediction did not exceed a
five-variable clinical model.

\section*{Introduction}

\textit{EGFR} genotype directs tyrosine kinase inhibitor (TKI) therapy in
non--small cell lung cancer (NSCLC). Tissue is often limited and molecular testing
is not universally available, so a non-invasive imaging surrogate would have
clinical value, and a large radiogenomics literature has followed.

That literature reports \textit{EGFR} prediction from CT at area under the
receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) 0.80--0.89
\citep{huang2025,shang2023,lai2026,wu2024,le2026}. The cohorts are typically
200--826 patients, usually single-center, and external validation is uncommon.
Against this, the one large study with external validation --- 1646 patients and
11\,473 segmented lesions with next-generation-sequencing labels --- reports
internal AUC 0.62--0.68 and external 0.55--0.63 \citep{rodriguez2026}. Its
external cohort is NSCLC-Radiogenomics, the collection used here.

That discrepancy is this study's motivation. Reported performance here is
sensitive to methodologic choices invisible at internal evaluation
\citep{maleki2022,horvat2024}, which makes separating the claims more informative
than another point estimate. We do not propose a better classifier. We separate
two claims that are routinely bundled --- that such a model \textit{localizes} the
lesion, and that it \textit{genotypes} it --- and test each with the controls it
requires, on a cohort where the second can be measured against the clinical
variables it would have to beat.

\section*{Materials and Methods}

\subsection*{Study design and cohorts}

The analysis plan, including the primary endpoint and all controls, was fixed and
version-controlled before external evaluation; deviations from it are logged in
the public repository accompanying this work.

The internal cohort is NSCLC-Radiogenomics \citep{bakr2018}, a surgical series
distributed by The Cancer Imaging Archive. Participant flow is in Figure 1: 211
patients, 188 with an imaging series, 158 preprocessed, 153 with a known
\textit{EGFR} call, 133 with adenocarcinoma, 97 with both adenocarcinoma and a
pixel-level segmentation.

No accrual window is reported: the collection's CT dates span 1989--1996 and are
de-identification offsets, so publishing a range derived from them would
misdescribe the cohort. The interval from index test to reference standard, which
survives the offset because it is a difference, has a median of 38 days
(IQR, 19.5--65.5).

The external cohort is NSCLC-Radiomics \citep{aerts2014}, 420 preprocessed
patients from a Dutch center --- another country, scanner fleet and clinical
population. The cohorts share no patients and no evaluated checkpoint was
warm-started from the external collection; both are asserted in the test suite
rather than claimed in prose.

\subsection*{Reference standard}

\textit{EGFR} status was determined by sequencing and is distributed with the
collection \citep{bakr2018}. The reference standard for attention is the expert
tumor segmentation distributed with each collection. Only one contour per lesion
exists, so interreader agreement cannot be computed; a mask perturbation sweep is
reported instead and is not a substitute.

\subsection*{Model}

The image encoder is the vision tower of BiomedCLIP (ViT-B/16, last two blocks
unfrozen), producing patch tokens per section, aggregated across 16 sections by a
masked mean. A region head applies eight learned queries by cross-attention, and
the resulting maps are supervised against the tumor region of interest (ROI) on
the patch grid. The classifier pools from those region embeddings, so prediction
and attention share a representation. A language decoder exists in the codebase
but its loss weight is zero in every run reported here and its output does not
reach the classifier; this is an image-only model.

\textbf{The attention task is ROI-conditioned, not whole-thorax detection.}
Inputs are axial crops of $(\text{bbox}_{\text{mm}} + 24)\times 2.5$ around the
lesion with 30\% positional jitter, windowed at center $-600$~HU and width
1500~HU, and resized to $224\times224$ pixels. The lesion sits near the center of
the field of view by construction, and the model is never asked to find a tumor in
a whole chest CT. A center prior is therefore a mandatory control, and the metrics
invariant to its width carry the argument. Because the attention maps are
supervised directly against the reference segmentation, we call this
mask-supervised attention rather than weakly supervised localization, which
denotes attention emerging from image-level labels alone.

\subsection*{Endpoints and controls}

H\textsubscript{1} (primary): external attention-mass lift over a per-fold spatial
shuffle is greater than zero. Three controls appear in every reported table:

\begin{itemize}\itemsep0pt
  \item \textit{Spatial shuffle} --- the same values permuted across cells,
    isolating \textit{where} mass fell from \textit{how peaked} the map is.
  \item \textit{Center prior} --- external ROI centroids sit at $(0.50, 0.51)$ of
    the frame covering 4.9\% of its area, so ``look at the middle'' is a strong
    baseline. Pointing accuracy and area-matched intersection over union are
    invariant to the prior's width, so that margin cannot be tuned.
  \item \textit{Weight randomization} --- an untrained head of identical
    architecture, ruling out inductive bias.
\end{itemize}

H\textsubscript{2} (co-primary, pre-declared null): $\Delta$AUC $=$
AUC(clinical $+$ model) $-$ AUC(clinical), where the combination is an unweighted
average of within-fold ranks, that is, soft voting. This topology was
pre-specified; the alternatives in Table 2 are a reported ablation and not the
endpoint.

The five-variable clinical model is a logistic regression on age, sex,
self-reported ethnicity, smoking status and pack-years. Numeric variables are
median-filled with a missingness indicator and categorical variables get an
explicit unrecorded level, so no patient is dropped and every arm is scored on the
same patients. Imputation, encoding and tuning occur inside the training folds.

\textbf{Endpoints and their populations.} H\textsubscript{1} uses the 420 external
patients; H\textsubscript{2} and every arm in Table 2 use the 153 internal patients
with a known \textit{EGFR} call. The 97-patient segmented-adenocarcinoma set is the
pre-specified analysis set for the histology-restricted sensitivity analysis below,
not for the headline figure.

\subsection*{Statistical analysis}

Evaluation used nested five-fold cross-validation (CV) stratified on
\textit{EGFR}, three seeds, with within-fold rank normalization before pooling.
The headline is the across-seed mean $\pm$ SD; a pooled confidence interval (CI)
absorbs no rerun variance and is never quoted alone. No operating point is
defined, because the calibration reported below does not support a threshold, so
sensitivity and specificity are not reported.

Comparisons used a paired bootstrap over patients (2000 resamples), with Holm
correction across the ten arms of the pooling sweep. Precision used the
Hanley--McNeil closed form \citep{hanley1982}, validated against a bootstrap;
minimum sample size follows Riley et al \citep{riley2020}, applied only where its
inputs are defined. Reporting follows CLAIM \citep{claim2020}, whose completed
checklist accompanies this manuscript.

\textit{P} values less than .05 indicated statistical significance, and all tests
were two-sided. Analyses were performed in Python 3.13.9 (Python Software
Foundation) with NumPy 2.1.3, SciPy 1.15.3, scikit-learn 1.6.1, pandas 2.2.3 and
statsmodels 0.14.4 (open-source, respective maintainers), and PyTorch 2.10.0
(Meta Platforms).

\subsection*{Use of artificial intelligence tools}

Generative artificial intelligence was used substantially in this work, and the
extent is stated rather than minimized. Claude Opus 5 (Anthropic), accessed
through the Claude Code command-line interface between July 20 and August 13,
2026, was used to write and refactor the analysis code, to execute and summarize
the reported analyses, to search the literature, and to draft and revise this
manuscript; 65 of the 68 commits in the accompanying repository record that
assistance in their metadata. The assistance also introduced errors that reached
reported numbers before review caught them, each recorded with its correction and
its regression test in the public version history.

The authors defined the research question, approved the pre-specified analysis
plan before external evaluation, verified every reported number against the

the integrity and accuracy of the work. No artificial intelligence tool meets
authorship criteria and none is listed as an author.

\section*{Results}

\subsection*{Patient characteristics}

Of 211 patients, 153 had a known \textit{EGFR} call and formed the analyzed sample
(mean age, 69 years $\pm$ 9 [SD]; 98 male, 55 female), of whom 40 (26.1\%) were
mutant. Never-smokers were 20 of 40 (50\%) of mutants against 13 of 113 (12\%) of
wild-type patients, and 133 of 153 (87\%) had adenocarcinoma (Table 1).

\subsection*{The model does not exceed the clinical variables}

Table 2 gives every arm on identical patients and folds. Pooled out-of-fold
\textit{EGFR} AUC was $0.618 \pm 0.025$ across three seeds. Smoking status alone
reached 0.794 and the five-variable clinical model 0.764.

The co-primary endpoint was $\Delta$AUC $= -0.029 \pm 0.015$, with every seed's CI
including zero. No fusion topology exceeded the clinical model: soft voting of the
model and clinical arms reached $0.736 \pm 0.005$, cross-fitted stacking
$0.718 \pm 0.014$, and hard voting $0.688 \pm 0.006$.

Radiomics helped neither arm, and the direction depends on which it is added to:
added to the clinical model it reduced AUC from 0.764 to 0.703 ($-0.061$;
\textit{P} $=$ .02 in all three seeds), while added to the imaging model it
changed nothing (0.618 to $0.619 \pm 0.015$; \textit{P} $=$ .87). A logistic
regression on frozen encoder features scored $0.617 \pm 0.052$, indistinguishable
from the full network to three decimals.

Results are shown per arm in Figure 2 and with paired differences in Figure 3.
\textit{KRAS} was at chance ($0.489 \pm 0.005$); Gevaert et al likewise report no
significant \textit{KRAS} model on this cohort \citep{gevaert2017}.

\subsection*{The predictions are also uncalibrated}

Calibration was assessed on the raw per-patient out-of-fold probabilities; the
within-fold rank normalization used for pooling is applied afterwards and never
enters these figures, because rank-normalized values are not probabilities.

Calibration slope averaged 0.19 against a perfect 1.0, so predictions are roughly
five times too extreme. At a base rate of 26.1\%, predicting the prevalence for
every patient gives a Brier score of 0.193; the model scored 0.236. The
probabilities are therefore worse than a constant, with resolution --- the
component only a better model can improve --- between 0.009 and 0.017. This is why
no operating point is pre-specified, and why any future externally validated
artifact must carry explicit recalibration.

\subsection*{On the subset that should carry it, performance is weaker}

Every \textit{EGFR}-mutant patient had adenocarcinoma; the 17 with squamous cell
carcinoma and three with NSCLC not otherwise specified were wild-type without
exception, making 20 of 153 (13\%) of the cohort classifiable from histology alone.
On the segmented-adenocarcinoma set ($n = 97$, 23 mutant) AUC falls to 0.563. The
same pattern appears in the literature: Yamazaki et al report 0.774 across
histologies but 0.687 restricted to adenocarcinoma \citep{yamazaki2022}.

\subsection*{Localization generalizes to an unseen cohort}

On 420 held-out NSCLC-Radiomics patients (Figure 5), attention mass inside the
tumor was 0.292 against 0.048 for the spatial shuffle --- a lift of $+0.244$,
exceeding the shuffle in 11 of 15 evaluations, which meets H\textsubscript{1} as
pre-specified.

\textbf{The shuffle is the weak control, and we do not rest the claim on it.} A
Gaussian blob fixed at the frame center scores 0.270 on mass against the trained
model's 0.292, so most of the margin over a shuffle is reproduced by looking at
the middle of a lesion-centered crop. The informative comparison is against that
center prior on the metrics it cannot exploit: pointing accuracy 0.609 versus
0.476 and area-matched intersection over union 0.394 versus 0.287, both invariant
to the prior's width. An untrained head of identical architecture returned a mass
lift of exactly $+0.000$ at a peak-to-mean ratio of 1.01 and pointing accuracy
0.005, ruling out architectural inductive bias.

\textbf{The fold count is a weak estimator, reported because it was
pre-specified.} The margin is one fold, 11 of 15 (73.3\%) against 71.4\%, and the
15 evaluations are not 15 studies: three seeds by five folds on the same 420
patients with overlapping training sets, so the count treats dependent replicates
as independent. A patient-level analysis is reported below as post hoc; we did not
substitute it for H\textsubscript{1}, because choosing a better endpoint after
seeing the data is the failure pre-specification prevents.

Table 3 reports every evaluation individually, and the distribution is bimodal
rather than marginal: the weakest success has pointing accuracy 0.757, the
strongest failure exactly 0.000, nothing between.

The failures are concentrated rather than scattered: one run aligned in two of five
folds while the others reached five of five and four of five, and that same run
aligns in two of five folds internally while carrying the highest classification
AUC of the three. \textbf{Attention alignment is therefore training-unstable}, and
nothing in the internal metrics predicts which run will transfer.

\textbf{Post hoc: the margin over the center prior depends on treating the
training run as fixed.} Resampling the 420 patients, pointing accuracy exceeded
the center prior by $+0.133$ (95\% CI: 0.082, 0.183; \textit{P} $<$ .001) and
overlap by $+0.107$ (95\% CI: 0.082, 0.133; \textit{P} $<$ .001). Resampling the
three training runs as well, both intervals include zero (\textit{P} $=$ .30 and
\textit{P} $=$ .19); only the contrast against an untrained head survives.
Between-run variance therefore dominates. A hierarchical interval on three runs is
wide by construction and underpowered at the run level, but that is the finding
rather than an excuse for it.

\textbf{Displacing the lesion separates the model from the prior directly.} We
translated each external crop, and its reference contour, so the tumor was no
longer centered, changing nothing else. Training used 30\% crop jitter, so 20\% is
inside that regime and 35\% is at the edge of where the lesion stays in frame.
Across the 11 aligning folds, pointing accuracy fell from 0.831 to 0.776 to 0.711;
the center prior fell from 0.476 to 0.140 to 0.002 (Table 3). A model attending to
the middle would track the prior; this one tracks the tumor.

\subsection*{The peritumoral region carries the signal, but not significantly}

Because the classifier pools from the attended region --- the lesion itself --- we
asked whether the \textit{EGFR} signal sits where the attention loss supervises.
Over ten seeds on frozen encoder features, mean-pooling the peritumoral ring
scored 0.695 against 0.632 for the whole crop and 0.629 for the tumor interior; a
size- and shape-matched ring at a \textit{random} location scored 0.575, so the
gain is location-specific. Adding a peritumoral branch moved the trained model to
$0.675 \pm 0.017$.

This does not survive correction and we report it as suggestive: across the ten
arms of the sweep the best has \textit{P}\textsubscript{Holm} $=$ .28, and end to
end the branch is worth $+0.058$ (95\% CI: $-0.008$, 0.128; \textit{P} $=$ .09).
It replicates six prior reports at ring widths from 1 to 15~mm
\citep{yamazaki2022,shang2023,wu2024,le2026,lai2026,huang2025}. What is new is the
tension: the attention loss supervises \textit{onto} the lesion, and the
classification signal appears to sit just outside it.

\subsection*{Analytic choice is worth more than the effect}

On identical features and estimator, the radiomics arm moved 0.136 in AUC across
six defensible preprocessing choices, and 0.066 between flat and nested CV --- both
larger than the effects this literature reports as findings, and both invisible to
internal evaluation \citep{maleki2022}.

\subsection*{More patients would not settle it}

Riley's criterion requires 414 patients for the five-variable clinical model alone
and 42\,362 for the 512-feature frozen probe; this cohort has 153. Neither arm is
identifiable at this size, which is why the frozen probe matching the model cannot
be read as evidence that the architecture is redundant. Precision, not power,
binds any external test: a 95\% CI half-width of 0.05 needs $n = 661$, while the
entire open world of \textit{EGFR}-labeled CT holds roughly 327 cases with 64
positives, giving 0.154 --- an interval spanning chance and the published 0.80s at
once.

\section*{Discussion}

Two claims usually bundled together separate cleanly here, and separate
differently. Mask-supervised attention stayed on the lesion in a cohort from
another country, scanner fleet and treatment setting. Genotype prediction did not
clear a five-variable clinical model on the cohort it was trained on
($\Delta$AUC $= -0.029 \pm 0.015$), was not rescued by any fusion topology, and was
worse than a constant on the probability scale (Brier score 0.236 against a
base-rate floor of 0.193).

Two limits bound what ``transferred'' means, and they resolve differently. The
task is ROI-conditioned, so this is alignment rather than detection --- but the
attention is not merely exploiting that conditioning: displacing the lesion to
35\% of the frame costs the aligning folds 0.12 of pointing accuracy while the
center prior loses essentially all of its (0.476 to 0.002). The binding limit is
the second. Transfer is training-unstable, and once the run is treated as a random
effect the margin over the center prior is no longer distinguishable from zero.
The dissociation below is a statement about the models we trained, not a guarantee
about the next one.

\textit{What the localization endpoint does and does not establish.} Attention
mass, pointing accuracy and area-matched intersection over union are not measures
of diagnostic accuracy, and no clinical claim follows from them directly. They
answer a narrower question that has been assumed rather than tested: whether the
explanation a model offers for its prediction is anchored to the lesion at all, on
data from an institution it has never seen. That question matters precisely
because the accompanying prediction fails. A saliency map that tracks the tumor on
an unseen cohort, while the classifier built on it performs at 0.618, is direct
evidence that plausible localization is not evidence of predictive validity --- a
dissociation that cannot be observed in a study where both are reported as
successes. We therefore present H\textsubscript{1} as a methodologic result about
interpretability claims, not as a clinical endpoint, and we do not propose the
localizer for clinical use.

\textit{Convergence with the largest external study.} Rodríguez Sánchez et al
\citep{rodriguez2026} provide the strongest argument that this is a property of
the problem rather than of our sample. Their 1646 patients and 11\,473 lesions,
with next-generation-sequencing labels and biopsy-anchored training, reproduce the
interval we obtain from 153 patients, and their external cohort is the collection
we use internally. Ten times the patients did not move the ceiling. With the
precision bound above --- a half-width of 0.154 on the largest test set the open
literature can assemble --- the field cannot resolve this question with public
data alone.

We are more cautious about what explains the reported 0.80s. Preprocessing choices
moved discrimination by 0.136 on identical features here, so analytic flexibility
is sufficient to produce such a gap --- but it does not follow that it is the
explanation in any particular study, and cohort composition, histologic mix, label
provenance, scanner differences and undetected leakage could each contribute. The
defensible claim is narrower: a reported AUC in this literature is not
interpretable without the preprocessing and validation protocol that produced it.

\textit{Comparison with prior reports.} Our peritumoral finding replicates six
prior reports in direction while failing significance after correction for the ten
arms examined --- a likelier description of that literature than the alternative,
given the same six reports place the optimal ring width anywhere from 1 to 15~mm.
Our histology result reproduces Yamazaki et al \citep{yamazaki2022}: performance
falls when the squamous patients, wild-type without exception, are removed. Any
study reporting \textit{EGFR} AUC across mixed histologies is partly reporting a
histology classifier.

\textit{Limitations.} The internal cohort is single-institution and historical,
with no recoverable accrual window. The classification arm has no external
validation, justified by the measured precision bound rather than by circumstance.
Interreader variability was not measured, because the source collections
distribute one contour per lesion; a mask perturbation sweep is reported instead
and is not a substitute. Acquisition parameters are incompletely recoverable. One
of three runs largely fails to align, and we report the endpoint across all three.
Finally, H\textsubscript{1} is confirmatory rather than a blind prospective test:
the external cohort was on disk, and the metric's behavior on it partly known,
when the threshold was set.

In conclusion, on this cohort mask-supervised tumor attention transferred across
institutions while \textit{EGFR} prediction from lesion-anchored CT did not exceed
routinely available clinical variables. Reports of AUC 0.80--0.89 should be read
against the analytic-choice sensitivity and precision bounds measured here.
